# Insights: Kyiv Pet Business Market (BigQuery)

> ⚠️ **Data limitations:** insights are based on a small sample and a short 
> time window — conclusions about seasonality or long-term trends are not 
> possible. The analysis reflects the visible part of Kyiv's pet business 
> market, manually collected from open sources. After self-registration for 
> businesses launches, category and geographic proportions are expected to 
> shift significantly.

## Insights

### Geographic distribution: central concentration, empty periphery
Most businesses are clustered in central and near-central Kyiv, while 
residential districts show only sparse or empty coverage. This is expected 
for manually collected data — businesses that are easier to find online 
have typically already invested in promotion. The real market is likely 
broader than the platform shows; vet clinics, pet shops, and grooming 
salons in residential areas probably exist but weren't captured due to 
weak online presence.

### Category distribution: vet clinics dominate
The platform lists **67 vet clinics, 33 pet shops, and 23 grooming salons**. 
The medical segment is the most saturated, leaving the most room for growth 
in the other two categories.

### Finding a checkup is easy — finding sterilization takes effort
Max number of services varies by business type: vet clinics offer up to 8, 
pet shops up to 3, grooming salons up to 4. Among vet clinics:
- **Most common:** checkups and surgery — near-universal, since a clinic 
  can't function without them
- **Least common:** sterilization/castration and X-ray — likely require 
  dedicated equipment, post-op stationary care, or specialist staff

### 68.7% of vet clinics offer emergency care
40.3% operate 24/7, 28.4% partially, and only 31.3% offer no emergency 
services at all. Decent coverage overall, but a third of clinics without 
emergency care remains a meaningful gap — response speed is critical for 
pet owners in a crisis.

### Grooming has the weakest coverage of exotic pets
- **Vet clinics:** 43 businesses serve both common and exotic pets — the most inclusive segment
- **Pet shops:** 31 of 32 serve exotic pets only as an add-on to common pets, not a specialization
- **Grooming salons:** only 1 of 23 serves exotic pets — practically absent

Owners of exotic pets (birds, rodents, reptiles) needing professional 
grooming have almost nowhere to go.

### Profile completeness
66 businesses have a perfect profile (5/5), 32 near-complete (4/5), but 20 
have only 3/5 and fewer at 2/5. Since data was collected manually rather 
than self-reported, low completeness at this stage reflects limited public 
information availability rather than business quality.

## Key Findings & Recommendations

**№1. Exotic pet grooming is an untapped niche.**  
Once the platform actively recruits businesses post-launch, prioritize 
grooming salons that specialize (or are willing to specialize) in exotic 
pets. First mover in this niche gains a clear competitive edge.

**№2. Expand geographically beyond the center.**  
Once self-registration is live, run local marketing campaigns in 
residential districts — local community groups, courtyard ads, 
partnerships with neighborhood pet shops — to fill in the map and make the 
platform useful citywide.

**№3. Add onboarding prompts for profile completion.**  
A LinkedIn-style progress bar during self-registration would raise the 
share of 5/5 profiles from day one rather than after the fact, building 
user trust immediately.

**№4. Explore "specialization" as a distinct business model.**  
Clinics with the most services (7–8) vs. the fewest (2–3) may represent 
two different strategies — generalist vs. specialist (e.g., dentistry- or 
surgery-only). Worth investigating in a future iteration: do specialized 
clinics earn higher ratings/trust than generalist ones? If so, a 
"Specialized Clinic" badge could become its own search filter, rather than 
using service count as a quality proxy.
